Turning a technician's spoken visit into a job record

What this is. A technician talks while working on several units. This prototype listens and keeps the job record as they go: each reading on the right unit, corrections applied, and approved work kept apart from work that is actually finished.

Why. Hands-free tools for technicians have to get exact numbers right from speech, often in noise. This project measures where that works and where it breaks.

What you are seeing. Practice visits written as scripts and read aloud by a computer voice, so the right answer is known for every line. On the left is what was said (▸ plays it). On the right is the record the system kept, updated after each line. The small numbers next to a value are the lines it came from. In the noisy visit, "heard" is what the speech recognizer actually wrote down.

Live mode. This page replays saved runs and makes no model calls. The same record also runs hands-free from a microphone on a Mac: you click Start once and talk, it updates about 3.5 seconds after you stop, and it asks out loud when a reading is unclear (see Results for a screenshot and how to try it).

Press Play or use the arrow keys. Tick "Check against ground truth" to mark each entry right or wrong.
